Project: First Impressions

“Today’s your lucky day. … Because it’s the day you get to meet Donna.”

Donna Paulsen, Suits
Goal

One desk for everything: create Donna’s project, connect Gmail and Google Calendar, and get your first drafted reply.

The steps

  1. Create the project

    In the Claude desktop app’s sidebar, click Projects → New project. Name it Donna.

    Everything we do today happens inside this project. One desk, one drawer, all the paperwork in it.

    You should see An empty project called Donna, with a Set project instructions option in the side panel. Leave the instructions alone for now. That’s Case File 02.

  2. Connect Gmail, Calendar, and Drive

    Click your initials in the bottom-left corner → Settings → Connectors. Then, one at a time:

    • Find Gmail and click Connect. Your browser opens a Google sign-in page. Choose your work account, then click Continue (some screens say Allow).
    • Do the exact same for Google Calendar.
    • And once more for Google Drive. Donna files her briefings there in Case File 03.

    Denise pre-enabled these for the whole workspace. All you ever click is Connect.

    You should see Back in Settings → Connectors: Gmail, Google Calendar, and Google Drive all read Connected.

    Connected, but Claude says it can’t see your inbox? Quit Claude completely and reopen it (Mac: ⌘Q · Windows: right-click the tray icon → Quit). That clears it up nine times out of ten.

    On Outlook instead of Gmail?

    Connect Microsoft 365 instead: one connection covers Outlook mail, your calendar, and OneDrive. Sign in with your work email, approve the usual MFA tap, click Accept. Everywhere this workshop says Gmail, Calendar, or Drive, read Outlook, Calendar, or OneDrive.

    Prefer a guide matched to your exact screen? Paste this into the project:

    Walk me through connecting my work email and calendar to Claude. First ask whether I’m on Gmail or Outlook, Mac or Windows, and whether IT manages my account. Check Anthropic’s current help docs before answering, then give me numbered steps naming the exact buttons, a way to verify it worked, and what to do if I hit an “administrator hasn’t approved this” error.

  3. Ask the question you’d ask your assistant

    Click into your Donna project and paste:

    Prompt 1: The Question 
    What landed in my inbox in the last 24 hours that actually needs ME?
Top 5, one line each.
    You should see Claude reading your actual inbox and coming back with five one-line items. This is the moment most people go quiet.

  4. Get your first draft, delivered where you live

    Prompt 2: The First Draft 
    Draft a reply to #1. Under 100 words, warm but decisive.
Save it as a draft in my Gmail so I can review it there.
Do not send it.
    You should see Open Gmail in a browser tab (or on your phone) and click Drafts. There’s a reply sitting there that you didn’t type.
You did it when…

A draft you didn’t write is waiting in your Gmail Drafts.

Stop

Hands off the keyboard here. We regroup as a room, then open Case File 02 together. Ahead of the group? Help a neighbor. It counts double.

Why this mattersA chat forgets when it ends. A project remembers: one desk, every conversation, all your context in the drawer.
