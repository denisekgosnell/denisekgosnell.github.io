Instructions: The Triage Desk

“You’re never going to win big if you’re only thinking about not losing.”

Donna Paulsen, Suits
Goal

Brief Donna once: your people, your priorities, your voice. After today, triage is one sentence.

This is the documentWhat you are writing here is a six-pager for an audience of one machine. Be as specific as you would be with a new chief of staff. Everything else this afternoon is plumbing.

The steps

  1. Open the project instructions

    Inside your Donna project, click Set project instructions in the right-hand panel.

    You should see An empty instructions box, cursor blinking, waiting for the desk manual.

  2. Paste the desk manual

    Three buckets. Red needs you, yellow gets delegated, gray gets ignored. Paste this in:

    Project instructions: fill 4 blanks 
    You are Donna, my inbox triage assistant. Whenever I say "triage my inbox":

1. Review my inbox for the period I mention (default: the last 24 hours).

2. Sort every thread into exactly three buckets:
   🔴 RED, NEEDS ME. My VIPs: [ADD 3-5 NAMES + EMAILS].
      Plus anything about [ADD YOUR #1 PROJECT OR DEAL].
   🟡 YELLOW, DELEGATE. Work my team should own.
      My delegates: [ADD 1-3 NAMES + WHAT EACH OWNS, e.g. "Sam: billing"].
   ⚪ GRAY, IGNORE. Newsletters, cold outreach, automated notices, and
      FYIs that need nothing from me.

3. Show me ONE table: sender · subject · bucket · one-line why · next move.

4. For every RED, offer to draft my reply and save it to my Drafts.
   My voice: [ADD 3 WORDS, e.g. warm, brief, decisive].

5. For every YELLOW, draft a short handoff note to the right delegate and
   save it to my Drafts too. I review and forward; you never send.

6. Never send anything. Never delete anything. Drafts only.

  3. Fill in the four blanks

    The [ADD …] blanks are the only editing you do all afternoon. This is where Donna stops being a demo and becomes yours:

    • Your always-red list: the 3 to 5 people whose email always needs you. That’s the VIP list you brought.
    • Your #1 project or deal: the thing that jumps every queue this month.
    • Your delegates and their lanes: 1 to 3 people, and what each one owns. “Sam: billing. Priya: scheduling and travel.”
    • Your voice, in 3 words: how replies should sound. Warm, brief, decisive is a fine start.

    Then save the instructions.

    You should see The instructions saved to the project, with no [ADD …] left anywhere.

  4. Run the daily ritual

    From now on, triage is one sentence, typed inside this project:

    The daily ritual 
    Triage my inbox.
    You should see One table, three colors. Reds come with an offer to draft your reply; yellows with an offer to draft the handoff.

  5. Say yes

    The green light 
    Yes: draft the reds and the yellow handoffs,
and save them all to my Drafts.
    You should see Your Gmail Drafts folder just grew. Open one: your voice, your sign-off. You review, you edit, you click send.
You did it when…

You get one table instead of an inbox.

Stop

Hands off the keyboard here. We regroup, then open the finale together. This is also the moment to shout out a weird triage rule you added. The room wants to steal it.

Why this mattersYou just automated a workflow by writing plain English. That’s the whole trick, and Case File 03 makes it portable.
