The short version

Executives do not scale by making more decisions. They scale by writing down how decisions get made, then letting other people run the document. That is what a press release and FAQ is at Amazon. That is what a six-page narrative is. Today you will run the same flywheel with one substitution: the document is a skill file, and the thing reading it is an agent instead of a room.

It also explains the risk. AI does not fix a broken process. It runs it faster. If the document is vague, you have hired something that will execute vagueness perfectly, all day. So we start by writing a good one.

What you’ll leave with

A working AI executive assistant that triages your inbox, drafts replies in your voice (and never sends without you), and delivers a daily briefing to your calendar, so an email comes to you exactly when you want it.

House rules

Donna never sends an email and never deletes one. Everything she writes waits in your Drafts for your click. You are, and remain, the send button.

We build in waves: every case file ends at a red stop sign where we regroup, so nobody gets left behind and nobody has to wait alone.

Four ways to use today

You will recognize yourself in one or two of these. All four are correct, and they change what you should optimize for in the room.

  • Accuracy Spend your time on the reasoning and write the most specific rules in the room. You leave with a skill file precise enough to defend in a review.
  • Time Get to the scheduled task, then come back and tighten the file. You leave with a running agent and a 4:54 briefing, today.
  • Trust Spend your time on voice, and on the delegate handoff notes. You leave with drafts your people would believe you wrote.
  • Vision Keep a running list of the other workflows worth automating. You leave with a repeatable method and your first three candidates.
